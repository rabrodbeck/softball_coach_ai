Q: What equipment does my player need?
A: A bat, a glove, a batting helmet, a fielding mask, softball pants, and cleats.  If your player wants to be a catcher it is probably best to get your own catcher's gear as any shared gear cannot be guaranteed to fit well.

Q: How long do games typically last?
A: It really depends on the league that you are playing in.  Most leagues follow a standard set of rules but also adopt some of their own or tweak them.  Typically, games have a rule that no new inning can start after an hour and forty five minutes with a drop dead time of 2 hours.  Playoff games may vary.

---

Q: How long do practices typically last?
A: A good practice time is an hour and a half.  An hour or less really isn't enough time to get some good work in.  Anything longer than an hour and a half is pretty draining and will tire the players out.  Remember, practices usually are go, go, go.  Whereas in a game, there is more "rest" time.

Q: What is the goal for the season?
A: This question varies based on the type of league.  All leagues should be focusing on having fun, developing skills, and growing as a player and teammate.  Travel leagues are more competitive and focus more on winning.  And travel players typically play one or two positions the entire time.  Rec leagues are more focus on skill development with less of an emphasis on winning.  Players in rec leagues typically move positions from inning to inning to they get to experience most positions.  No matter the situation, it's softball and should be fun.

Q: How should I handle team communication throughout the season?
A: For a coach, this is a matter of personal preference.  There are many ways to communicate with the team/parents such as email, group texts, or social media.  GameChanger is wildly popular app that I prefer to use.  You can put your practice and game schedule in there and it each player/parent can RSVP to events so you will know ahead of time who will and who won't be there.  It also has a built-in messaging system too.  The important thing to remember here is that we are talking about young players who have to rely on parents for rides.  We all want to think the players are growing up and becoming responsible, but that's not always the case.  Any important communication should be made to the players as well as the parents.

Q: How do you help a hitter who is afraid of fast pitching?
A: There are two main reasons a hitter may fear a fast pitcher.  The first is getting hit by a pitch.  They see the speed of the pitch and automatically become worried about getting hurt.  This is just something that girls have to get used to on their own.  They need to understand that they will likely get hit by a pitch at some point and it is going to hurt.  It is a risk that they should know about.  The second reason is because they lack confidence in hitting a fast pitcher.  Knowing a fast pitcher that you can borrow for a couple of practices would be a good way to get them experience.  Lacking that, a coach could set up a net for safety about 15-20 feet away from the plate and toss some quick pitches to the batter.  Sadly, the only true way to get over a fear of facing a fast pitcher is to just get in the box and see what you can do.
