DRILL: Imaginary Pitch
CATEGORY: Hitting
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 10-15 minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 2+

SETUP:
The players stand in a circle around the coach and assume a hitting stance, making sure they have plenty of room to fully swing their bat without hitting anyone.

INSTRUCTIONS:
The coach goes into a wind-up and pretends to throw a pitch, while saying something like "outside corner strike", "low and inside strike", "high ball", etc.

COACHING CUES:
- Players need to make sure they are swinging where the pitch is "thrown".  If coach calls "low and inside strike", then the players should be swinging to hit a low and inside pitch.
- Players need to listen and pay attention to ensure they are only swinging at strikes.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Not swinging at all on strikes.
- Not swinging in the proper area of strikes.
- Swinging at balls.

VARIATIONS:
- Make a game out of it.  Slowest player to swing at a strike is eliminated.  Any player swinging at a ball is eliminated.  Last player standing is the winner.

NOTES:
Helps hitters visualize a pitch being thrown and determining to swing or take.

DRILL: Soft-Toss
CATEGORY: Hitting
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 10-30 minutes depending on number of players
PLAYERS NEEDED: 1+

SETUP:
One hitter at the plate at a time, rest of team is fielding.  Hitter stands at the plate normally with a coach down on one knee about 4-5 feet away from them on the side of home plate.

INSTRUCTIONS:
Coach softly tosses balls to the hitter so that they begin at the top of the strike zone and fall towards the front of the plate.  Hitter hits the ball on the downward arc.

COACHING CUES:
- Focus on watching the ball as it starts to drop in the strike zone.
- Timing is critical here since the ball is not coming at them from the normal direction.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Many hitters struggle with this at first because they are not used to seeing a pitch fall from the top of the strike zone to the bottom while being over the plate the entire time.  
- Players getting upset that they find it difficult to make contact.  Let them know that it is a difficult drill to get the hang of and to not get discouraged.
-

VARIATIONS:
- If you there are fielders, have them make a play with every hit even though the hitter is not running.  Anything in the infield, have them throw to first.  Anything in the outfield, have them work on hitting the cutoff and getting the ball back in quickly.
- Every so often, let the hitter know to run on the next hit.  Don't let the fielders know the hitter will be running.
- For more advanced hitters, have them focus on hitting certain directions.  Try getting them to pull a few, hit a few up the middle, and hit a few to the opposite field.

NOTES:
This is a good drill for players to get many good swings in a short period of time.

DRILL: Ground Hog
CATEGORY: Hitting
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 30-45 minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 5+

SETUP:
One hitter at a time with the remaining players in the field.  All pitches come from a coach.

INSTRUCTIONS:
Hitter remains up to bat as long as they hit ground balls or line drives.  If a hitter pops it up or strikes out, their turn is over.  If a hitter gets up to 5 hits in a row, have them run out the next hit.  If they make it to first base safely, they remain at bat and their streak continues.

COACHING CUES:
- Hitter should focus on staying on top of the ball and driving it.
- Hitter should focus on a nice level swing.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Getting underneath the ball and hitting a pop up.

VARIATIONS:
- Keep track of each hitter's score and declare the winner "The Groundhog".

NOTES:
This drill can be done with only a handful of players.  If there are not enough players to field at least the infield, remove the part about running to first base after 5 hits and just focus on staying on top of the ball and hitting ground balls or line drives.

DRILL: Around the Horn
CATEGORY: Fielding
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 20-30 minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 4+

SETUP:
Divide the players up into 4 groups and place each group at a base.  It is ok if you do not have an equal number of players at each base as long as each base has at least one player.  Only one player should be at a base at a time, but the rest should be close by and ready to jump in.

INSTRUCTIONS:
Starting at home, make continuous throws around the horn (home -> 3rd -> 2nd -> 1st -> home...repeat).  After a player throws to the next base, the next player in line steps in and awaits the throw.  Players who make a bad throw has to run after it while coach puts a new ball in play.  Players who do no catch a good throw has to run after it while coach puts a new ball in play.


COACHING CUES:
- Make good throws, goal is to have the person catching the ball have a nice and easy catch.
- After a catch is made, make sure the player who caught it is setting themselves properly before making the next throw.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Rushing a throw

VARIATIONS:
- Keep track of how many times the ball gets around the horn without touching the ground.  Try to beat it the next time around.
- Get a stopwatch and time how long it takes them to get around the horn with everyone throwing and catching at least once.  Try to beat that time the next go around.
- Change things up and randomly call out "reverse".  Now the throws should be counter clockwise around the horn.

NOTES:
Key here is making good throws and catches, while trying to keep the ball constantly moving.  Depending on the age level, some players may not be able to throw the ball from one base to another in the air.  If that's the case, then focus on the throw still being on target even if it has to bounce or roll to get there.

DRILL: Slow Rollers
CATEGORY: Fielding
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 15-30 minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 3+

SETUP:
Start with one dedicated 1st baseman.  Split the remaining players between 3rd base (or shortstop) and home.  Each player at home should have a ball.

INSTRUCTIONS:
Players at home are the "rollers".  One at a time, the rollers should roll a slow grounder to the fielders who must charge the ball and make a throw to 1st base.  After the fielder makes the throw to 1st base, they should run to 1st base to retrieve their ball from the 1st baseman and go to the end of the rollers line.  The player who rolled the ball then runs to the end of the fielders line.

COACHING CUES:
- Be aggressive and charge in hard for the ball
- If the ball stops rolling, or is going very slowly by the time the fielder reaches it, they should pick the ball up with their barehand.
- Charging the ball is only half the battle, fielders still need to make a good throw to 1st base without stopping and setting themselves, they should be throwing on the move.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Not charging the ball hard enough and letting it come to them.
- Charging the ball correctly, but then slowing down and taking too long to throw the ball.

VARIATIONS:
- Make a game out of it by seeing how long they can go without making an error.

NOTES:
Make sure to rotate the 1st baseman every so often so they get a chance to be a roller and a fielder as well.

DRILL: Live Situations
CATEGORY: Fielding
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 30+ minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 6+

SETUP:
Keep one player in as a runner and put the rest on the field in whatever positions you want.  If there aren't enough girls to cover every position, put them wherever you want to focus on and rotate them around as needed.

INSTRUCTIONS:
Coach hits a ball to various places on the field as if it was a live game.  Player that is running takes off for 1st base when the ball is hit.  Fielders then try to make a play on the runner.  Or, you can start off with runners at any base(s) that you want.  Coach should call out a situation before every hit ("2 outs girls on 2nd", "bases loaded, nobody out".  After each play, review what went well and what did not.

COACHING CUES:
- Pay attention to the called situation.
- Know where you are going to throw the ball if it is hit to you.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Fielders aren't paying attention to the situation and make a poor decision.
- All fielders should be moving on every play and not just standing flat footed if the ball isn't hit towards them.

VARIATIONS:
- Put players only in the infield to focus on just infield work.
- Put players in the outfield, 2nd base, and shortstop to work on hitting the cutoff and getting the ball back in quickly.

NOTES:
Run through very common situations that occur in just about every game so that these things become routine.  Then focus on situations that you feel the team struggles with.

DRILL: Relay Race Around the Bases
CATEGORY: Baserunning
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 5-10 minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 6+

SETUP:
Split the players up into 2 teams.  If there is an odd number of girls, the team with less will have to designate one runner to go twice (typically first and last) for their team.  One team lines up at 2nd base and the other at home plate. The first runner for each team has a ball in their hands.

INSTRUCTIONS:
On "go" the race begin and each runner begins running the bases, finishing at the base they started at.  Once they reach their starting base, they must hand the ball to the next runner in line and that runner then runs around the bases.  First team to get all players back to their starting base wins.

COACHING CUES:
- Run hard.
- Make good turns at each base.
- Do not drop the ball.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Missing a base.
- Dropping a ball.
- Not making good turns at each base.

VARIATIONS:
- Run best 2-out-of-3.
- Come up with different "penalties" for a dropped ball.

NOTES:
Make sure the runners that are in line are far enough away that the opposing team can make a good turn at the base.  Once the opposing runner crosses, the next runner should step up and be ready to receive the ball and take off.  The ball should always be handed off and not tossed or thrown.

DRILL: 2nd Base Decision
CATEGORY: Baserunning
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 30 minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 7+

SETUP:
One player at each infield position (you could choose to not utilize a pitcher or 1st baseman). One field in left field and one in right field.  Any remaining players start as runners.

INSTRUCTIONS:
Coach will throw fly balls and ground balls to left, left-center, and center.  The runner on 2nd base has to decide whether to go to 3rd, to try and score, to tag-up and advance to 3rd, or to return to 2nd base.  

COACHING CUES:
- Make sure all players know what tagging-up means.
- Encourage runners to not run into outs and to make smart decisions.
- Teach runners to read the ball and know when it's safe to run.
- Teach the fielders about cutoffs and hitting the cutoff.
- Teach the fielders to communicate so the outfield/cutoff knows where the throw should go.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Getting doubled up on second when a fly ball is caught.
- Not reading balls correctly and making poor decisions.

VARIATIONS:
- Make a game of it.  A runner gets 1 point for correctly returning to 2nd base.  2 points for safely advancing to 3rd base.  -1 point for being thrown out.  -2 points if they are doubled up.

NOTES:
Rotate players around so everyone gets a chance to field and to run.  This is a good drill to use to help teach about tagging up on a fly ball.

DRILL: Through, Round, Go
CATEGORY: Baserunning
AGE GROUP: 8-14
DURATION: 15-20 minutes
PLAYERS NEEDED: 1+

SETUP:
All players are runners and should line up near Homeplate with their batting helmets.  Each player will run to 1st base one by one.

INSTRUCTIONS:
Runner takes off from home towards 1st base.  As they approach 1st, coach yells out either "Through", "Round", or "Go".
"Through" means the runner should run through 1st base hard.
"Round" means the runner should hit 1st and round the base a few feet toward 2nd.
"Go" means the runner should hit 1st hard and run to 2nd base.

COACHING CUES:
- Make sure when runners run through 1st base they turn towards foul territory instead of towards 2nd.
- Even when running "Through", runners should still be prepared to advance to 2nd in the case of a wild throw.
- When "Round" is called, make sure the runner makes the turn to 2nd and is in a good position to either continue on to 2nd or to get back to 1st base.

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Running through 1st but turning toward 2nd and getting tagged out.
- Rounding to much and getting caught off base.
- Not making a proper turn on a round or go call.

VARIATIONS:
- Make it a game by putting some fielders in position and utilize this in a game-like situation.  Let the play on the field help determine the "Through", "Round", or "Go".

NOTES:
Coaches can use whatever words or signals they choose to for this drill.  Some younger age groups would likely benefit from the verbal cues and utilize signals as they grow older.  If utilizing signals, make sure the runners know to look at their 1st base coach to get the signal.
